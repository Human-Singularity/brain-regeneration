ECTRIMS 2026 had its patient community day this past Friday. What follows are the main bullet points of what was discussed in the online stream.

## Part 1: MS, NMOSD, and MOGAD clinical updates

### Welcome and Opening Remarks

- First Patient Community Day in North America, held in Toronto as a joint ECTRIMS/ACTRIMS event
- Over 4,000 registrations: record attendance, both in-person and online
- Key theme: bridging patient lived experience with research and clinical expertise
- Every session featured people with lived experience on stage asking panelist questions
- First year with a dedicated NMOSD/MOGAD session and a closing 1-hour livestream Q&A

### CAR-T Cell Therapy

- Experimental therapy borrowed from oncology, currently accessible only via clinical trials
- Three delivery approaches:
    - Autologous: patient’s own cells extracted, engineered, reinfused
    - Allogeneic (“off the shelf”): donor cells engineered and given to patient
    - In vivo: proteins administered to engineer cells inside the body directly
- Key advantage: penetrates the CNS, reaching cells hidden in the brain that conventional drugs cannot
- Potential dual benefit in MS:
    - Eliminates relapse-driving cells while allowing new healthy cells to regenerate
    - May impact progressive MS mechanisms via CNS penetration
- Known risks: inflammatory toxicity, requirement for pre-treatment chemotherapy
- “Basket studies” now enrolling patients with MS, NMOSD, myasthenia gravis, and demyelinating neuropathies in a single trial design

### BTK Inhibitors

- ~7-8 BTK inhibitors under study in MS, each with slightly different drug properties
- Mechanism: inhibits BTK molecule in B cells and myeloid cells, reducing activation both peripherally and in the CNS
- Trial updates presented at MS Toronto 2026:
    - Tolebrutinib: positive in secondary progressive MS; approved in Europe, uncertain US status after FDA issues
    - Fenibrutinib: positive in relapsing MS and non-inferior to ocrelizumab in primary progressive MS
        - New MRI data: reduced slowly expanding lesions (SELs) and paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs)
    - Remibrutinib: clearly reduced relapses and new MRI lesions vs. teriflunomide
        - Disability endpoints showed trend toward benefit but did not reach statistical significance
        - Notably, no liver safety signal observed (a concern with earlier BTK inhibitors)
- Liver toxicity has been a class-wide concern; remibrutinib data is encouraging on this front

### Treatment De-escalation and Discontinuation

- Growing evidence base on when and how to safely reduce or stop MS treatment
- De-escalation: starting high-efficacy, stepping down; or spacing out high-efficacy dosing to reduce side-effect risk
- Stopping treatment:
    - Not recommended in younger patients on moderate-efficacy drugs: relapse risk returns
    - May be appropriate in older patients on milder therapies, on a case-by-case basis
    - Aging immune system naturally reduces relapse frequency and new lesion formation
- Registry studies and randomized trials both contributing data
- AI and statistical modeling being explored to individualize discontinuation decisions

### Myelin Repair and EBV Prevention

- Myelin repair (remyelination):
    - Oligodendrocytes are the target cells; many compounds identified in animal/lab models
    - 1-2 trials showing early positive biomarker signals (electrophysiology, MRI velocity measures)
    - Challenge: no trial yet shows robust clinical recovery; trial design for repair endpoints still evolving
    - Significant industry interest; meaningful results expected within 3-4 years
- EBV and MS prevention:
    - EBV is required but not sufficient for MS development
    - Growing evidence EBV may also drive relapses and progressive mechanisms
    - Prevention trials are complex: large populations, long follow-up needed
    - Infectious mononucleosis as an earlier, more tractable endpoint for vaccine trials
    - First-degree relatives (~3-5% MS risk) identified as a tractable high-risk trial population
    - Moderna studying an EBV vaccine in people already living with MS to prevent relapses

### NMOSD and MOGAD Updates

- NMOSD pre-clinical phase:
    - AQP4 antibodies detectable years before clinical onset
    - At that stage, antibodies present but no astrocyte damage yet
    - Significance of early antibody detection and whether to treat is an open question
- CAR-T in NMOSD: early Phase 1 data positive; multiple trials starting; Phase 3 potentially launchable in 2-3 years if results hold
- Relapse management in NMOSD:
    - Debate: methylprednisolone alone vs. adding plasma exchange vs. complement inhibitors
    - Panel view: combination of all anti-inflammatory approaches likely optimal to limit disability
    - Access gaps remain: plasma exchange not always available on weekends or at all centers
- MOGAD treatment advances:
    - METEORITE trial (satralizumab, anti-IL-6): first proven preventive treatment, Phase 3
    - TOMATO trial (tocilizumab, anti-IL-6 receptor): positive results in Chinese cohort, more globally accessible
    - Both target the IL-6 pathway; mechanism validated across two agents
    - No approved treatments yet in the US; approvals anticipated as trials complete
    - MOGAD attacks tend to recur at the same anatomical site (optic nerve or spinal cord), raising disability risk with each relapse
- Treatment choice in MOGAD: shared decision-making on route (injection vs. infusion), frequency, and lifestyle fit; cross-trial comparison difficult due to different designs

### Outlook and Key Messages

- NMOSD: 3-4 approved therapies now available in many regions; 10 years ago there were none
- MOGAD: first preventive treatments on the horizon; multiple additional trials underway
- Research is accelerating by applying lessons from the MS treatment landscape
- Biomarkers (GFAP, NfL) increasingly useful for monitoring disease activity in NMOSD/MOGAD
- Panelists emphasized gratitude to patients participating in trials: multi-day travel, repeated assessments, all voluntary

## Part 2: remyelination, aging, brain health, and lived experience

### Remyelination: Where the Science Stands

- Trials over the past 10 years confirm remyelination is achievable in humans
    - Signals seen in visual nerve conduction speed and MRI myelin content
    - No clear link to disability yet: trials run only 3-6 months, too short to show neuroprotection
- Benefit is greatest earlier in disease course
    - Fewer intact nerve fibers remain as MS progresses, reducing the magnitude of effect
    - Clearer results in relapsing MS vs. progressive MS so far
    - Still considered beneficial in progressive MS, just harder to demonstrate
- Metformin trial deployed to rejuvenate aged stem cells and extend remyelination benefit across age groups
- Remyelination agents may also function as senotherapeutics, slowing biological aging
    - Chris McMurrin (Cambridge) showed one repurposed drug slowed brain aging on MRI

### Aging, Biological Age, and MS

- Biological age diverges from chronological age in MS: disease appears to accelerate aging
    - Metabolic and other aging markers give a consistent picture of accelerated aging in some patients
- Disease course tends to shift around age 50-55: fewer relapses, less inflammatory MRI activity, but higher risk of progressive disability
- De-escalation or extended dosing of B-cell/infusion therapies is a valid strategy from ~50-55
    - Should be individualized based on biological age, not calendar age
- 60% of MS patients aged 60 in one Dutch cohort had undiagnosed high cholesterol
    - Highlights how comorbidities accumulate unnoticed alongside MS
- Sit-to-stand tests proposed as better measures of physical aging than standard walking speed

### Hormones, Lifespan, and Pregnancy

- Lifespan milestones (puberty, perimenopause, pregnancy) all influence how MS expresses
    - Applies to men and boys too, not only women
- Perimenopause is a multi-year process, not a single event; increasingly recognized as influencing MS course
    - HRT data presented: at minimum safe, potentially beneficial for MS course and symptoms
- Females tend to have higher relapse rates from puberty to midlife; males show earlier progression risk
- Pregnancy data is now highly reassuring
    - Third trimester naturally suppresses relapses
    - Postpartum treatment (1-3 weeks after birth) now well-supported by data
    - Rates of pregnancy in MS are rising: a direct result of improved safety confidence

### MS Prodrome and Early Identification

- Prodrome = the period before first recognizable MS symptom onset
    - Up to 15 years before onset, patients more likely to visit physicians for ill-defined symptoms
    - Fatigue, IBS, restless legs, anxiety, and depression elevated ~10+ years before onset
    - Physical activity starts dropping ~15 years before onset, with a second drop ~4-5 years before
- Psychiatric prodromal features associated with greater disability progression after MS onset
- Field is not yet able to accurately identify pre-MS individuals, but a pathway is emerging
    - Goal: enroll prodromal individuals in remyelination or prevention trials

### Brain Health, Exercise, and Comorbidities

- WHO recommends 150 minutes of activity per week; literature supports ~2x1hr sessions for measurable benefit
    - Benefits seen in balance, muscle strength, and movement
    - Aerobic and resistance training can protect or even strengthen brain volume
- Exercise does not have to be intensive to count
    - Walking slightly faster than usual, or raising heart rate modestly, shows brain benefit
    - Dual-tasking (cognitive + motor) helps restore efficient brain connectivity
    - Breaking exercise into 10-15 minute chunks throughout the day is a valid strategy
- Cardiovascular disease is underdiagnosed and undertreated in MS, and linked to faster disability progression
- Comorbidities (anxiety, depression, thyroid disease, IBD, cardiovascular disease) frequently co-occur with MS and complicate disease management

### Lived Experience Panel: Key Themes

- Personalized medicine is the shared aspiration: one-size-fits-all treatment algorithms do not reflect real-world MS
    - “Average patient problem”: trial cohorts often skew younger, healthier, and whiter than the real MS population
- Shift toward patient-centric research is underway but needs to go further
    - People with lived experience should be embedded at the study design stage, not just recruited as participants
    - Societies and patient organizations can connect individuals to researchers
- Strong call to participate in clinical trials: the only way research advances is through patient participation
- Stem cell therapy: replacing lost neurons is not yet achievable
    - Current focus: harnessing the brain’s own stem cells and making the environment more youthful
    - Preclinical (animal) work by groups like Piccino et al. shows myelin regeneration is possible
- Closing message from all three panelists: overwhelming hope
    - MS care today is unrecognizable from 15 years ago
    - Work is happening across remyelination, prevention, brain health, and quality of life simultaneously
    - “There has never been a better time to be alive with a chronic disease”
